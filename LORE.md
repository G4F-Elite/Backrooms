## The Pitch

Three friends and an intern found a way into the Backrooms and did the only reasonable
thing: they founded a company to steal the furniture.

The Backrooms are an infinite, unattended office. Everything in it is slightly impossible -
lamps that never burn out, clocks that are wrong in a consistent way - and therefore worth
money. The business plan fits on a whiteboard: **person -> door -> box -> coins**.

It works for exactly one shift.

The horror is real. The crew is here for the money and is not taking it seriously enough.
That gap is the whole game. It is a friendslop co-op: up to four players, a shared wallet,
and every mistake has a name attached.

---

## The Startup

- **Office:** a garage. Wood floor, white walls, a roller door that stays shut, pizza boxes.
  It is the only place in the game that does not look like the Backrooms.
- **Product:** a portal rig bolted around a doorway in the back wall. Home is on one side,
  yellow wallpaper on the other.
- **Phase two:** a drill, parked on the far side of the portal. The idea was to go one floor
  down, where the good stuff is. It is a prototype. It has loose cables and an open toolbox.
- **Staff:** you. Nickname, colour, and a reputation from the last shift.

Nobody has a tragic backstory. Somebody has a student loan.

## Day One (tutorial)

The first shift is safe. Nothing hunts the crew; the lights are on; the way home is right
behind them. The job is to carry three things back to the pallet in the garage, and each
one teaches a tool:

| Delivery | Where the thing is | What it teaches |
|----------|--------------------|-----------------|
| 1 | on the carpet, right past the portal | pick up, carry, deliver |
| 2 | in a wing where the lamps are dead | the flashlight |
| 3 | sealed behind a cracked wall | the scanner sees through walls; cracked walls come down |

Every delivery stays on the pallet as a cardboard box. Empty outlines show what is still owed.

Then the scanner finds one more thing. It glows. It is worth more than everything on the
pallet together.

---

## The Accident

Whoever carries the glowing thing to the portal drops it in the doorway.

White flash. The lights go out. When they come back the doorway is a wall with a burnt
arch on it, the rig is scrap, and the whole crew is on the wrong side - including anyone
who was standing in the garage, because the portal took them along on its way out.

In co-op the squad knows exactly who was holding it. This is intended.

From here on the floor is awake: things walk the halls, the walls move, the clock runs.

## The Drill

The drill is the only machine the crew still owns, and the only way to go anywhere. It is
how every floor ends. It is also where the crew lives now: a site trailer on crawler tracks with an
auger bolted to the front and a break room in the back.

- **It does not stop.** The Accident welded the clutch. It crawls across the floor in a
  straight line, slower than a person crouching, and eats whatever wall is in the way.
  The tunnel it leaves behind is the one landmark that never moves.
- **The break room** has a couch, fairy lights, a plush, a kettle and a wood floor. With the
  garage gone it is the only place that does not look like the Backrooms. Sitting in it
  brings morale back, patches people up a little and tops up flashlights. A charger on the
  wall finishes a battery every minute or so. A radar on the desk shows the loot around
  the rig to whoever is home.
- **Nothing comes near it while it runs.** Lamp on, engine putt-putting: the things in the
  halls keep well away from the whole machine and cannot see or hear anyone near it. They
  do not even hang around in the next hall over: around a running drill the floor is just
  an empty office, with no growling down the corridor and nothing going bump. This is the only safe place on the floor, and it is moving away from the loot.
- **It breaks down. Constantly.** Roughly once a minute, more often the deeper it is. It
  stops, the lamp drops to the spare battery, and only the break room itself is still safe.
  Half a minute later the spare is dead too: no lamp, no haven, and the engine starts
  backfiring loud enough to be heard across the floor. One thing is wrong each time - the
  fuse box by the door, or one of the two engine covers outside - and somebody has to go
  and hold a wrench on it.
- The front is the pointy end. Standing in front of it is a workplace injury.

- It runs on loot. Things go into the hopper and do not come back.
- The gauge on its side has five lamps. Each lamp is a fifth of what it needs for this floor.
- The hopper, the gauge and the lever are inside, on the wall between the break room and
  the engine. Feeding the drill means coming home.
- When all five are lit the rig parks, and somebody pulls the lever. Everything shakes, the
  floor gives way, and the whole squad - and whatever they are still carrying - arrives one
  floor down, sitting in the break room, under a fresh hole in the ceiling. Then it starts
  crawling again.
- Deeper floors want more. Deeper floors also hold better loot.
- A lamp coming on is progress, and progress pulls dead teammates back into the shift -
  they wake up on the couch.
- New loot keeps turning up ahead of the rig, and what was left far behind it is gone.
  Staying put is not a plan; neither is wandering off.

Down is the only direction the crew can afford.

## The Other Startup

The crew is not the first company with this idea. Starting one floor down there is a stall:
striped awning, a counter, somebody in a hood with a headlamp who never stands up, and a
winch with a rope that goes all the way up through the ceiling.

The rivals are not enemies. They are a business, which is worse:

- They **buy** anything the crew carries, at a fair price.
- They **sell** batteries, med spray and plush toys.
- They pay for **field notes**: point the scanner at one of the things in the halls, keep it
  in view for a few seconds, and the wallet goes up. Once per kind, per floor.
- They will **winch the whole squad out**. That is the end of the game, the good one.
  It costs more than the crew will see for several floors.

So every piece of loot is a choice: into the drill (deeper, richer, worse), or over the
counter (cash now, the rope home some day).

## The Loot

Office junk that should not exist. Value grows with depth; weight does not.

| Thing | Why it is worth money | Weight |
|-------|----------------------|--------|
| Lamp tube | still lit, not plugged into anything | light |
| Wallpaper roll | the yellow. People pay for the yellow | light |
| Wall clock | wrong, but always by the same amount | light |
| Red phone | it rings | light |
| CRT monitor | shows a room the crew has not found yet | medium |
| Almond water | the one thing down here that is good for you | medium |
| Safe | found inside walls. Nobody put it there | heavy |
| The core | glows. See: The Accident | heavy |

A crew member carries four things. Weight slows them down and drains their legs faster,
so the one hauling a safe is also the one who cannot outrun anything.
Going down means dropping everything where you fell.

## Digging

There is no money for the rope, so the crew digs.

- **Lying around:** the easy pieces, mostly near the drill.
- **Junk piles:** bags, cartons, fallen ceiling tiles. Rummaging takes a moment and makes
  noise. Usually there is something under it. Sometimes two things. Sometimes a battery.
- **Furnished rooms** (offices, server rooms, stores): the good stuff, and whatever comes
  with disturbing it.
- **Cracked walls:** some walls are cracked, and some cracks have metal glinting in them.
  Knocking a wall out takes a while, is the loudest thing a person can do down here, opens
  a shortcut, and now and then drops a safe on the carpet.
- **The scanner** shows loot and junk piles through walls. It is also the hand the
  flashlight would be in.

---

## Company Equipment

Everything was bought with the seed money, which is why there is so little of it.

- **Flashlight:** runs about a minute, recovers when switched off. It is the only thing
  that counts as "looking" in a blackout, and the building notices light.
- **Scanner:** finds loot, takes field notes. Held on a target too long it overheats and
  sulks until it cools. It was the cheapest one.
- **Batteries:** a couple always turn up near the crew. Nobody asks who leaves them.
  More at the stall, and one at a time from the charger in the break room.
- **Med spray:** stall only. Fixes legs, not decisions.
- **Plush toy:** see Morale.

---

## Morale

The crew calls it morale. It is sanity.

- It drains slowly just from being down here, faster near the things in the halls, faster
  on deeper floors.
- Low morale: flickers, shapes, steps, whispers that are not there. At zero the employee
  sits down on the carpet and stops working, same as being knocked down.
- **The plush toy** is the company's entire mental-health budget. Holding it brings morale
  back. A downed employee with a plush in the pocket gets up
  alone - the plush does not survive this.
- The rivals sell plush toys. Of course they do.

## Coworkers You Did Not Ask For

The crew's theory is that the things in the halls are previous startups that never exited.
Each follows exactly one rule. Learn the rule, then abuse it. Once the squad has taken
field notes on one, its rule stays on screen as a single pictogram.

### The Stalker - *eye*
- **Rule:** moves only while nobody has it on screen.
- **Handling:** somebody gets staring duty while the others haul. In a blackout only a
  flashlight counts as looking.

### The Crawler - *muted speaker*
- **Rule:** blind. Hears footsteps, sprinting, squeaks, callouts, shoves, pings, rummaging
  and, above all, walls coming down. Stops, listens, charges in a straight line.
- **Handling:** crouch or freeze. Sidestep. Line it up with a wall and it knocks itself out.

### The Shadow - *two people*
- **Rule:** goes after whoever wandered off alone, from where they are not looking.
- **Handling:** stick together. A glance sends it away; a long stare makes it angry -
  which is exactly what taking field notes on it requires.

### General Notes
- They only know what they saw or heard. None of them knows where you are - you told them.
- After a rough moment they back off. After too much quiet, one is sent to check on you.
- None of them will walk into the light of the running drill. All of them will walk into
  a dead one.
- The louder the crew, the sooner that check happens.

## The Building Has Opinions

The Backrooms is an office, and an office has facilities management. Nobody has met it.
It keeps a running tab of how much noise and light the crew makes - sprinting, squeaking,
flashlights - and answers in escalating order:

| How loud | What the building does |
|----------|------------------------|
| annoying | every phone on the floor starts ringing (B1). The ringing is noise too |
| rude | B1: the breaker is flipped, lights out. Deeper: the walls move behind the crew. Either way a Crawler is sent |
| unbearable | a Shadow is sent, and keeps being sent |

It also does things on its own schedule, loud crew or not:

- **Lights out.** A whole area goes dark for a while.
- **Walls move.** The corridor behind the crew is not the corridor they came through.
  The drill, the stall and the pallet are never walled off.
- **A doorway that is not one.** Looks open, is not, for a few seconds.
- **Holes in the floor.** They open near the crew - never under anyone's feet - and close
  again. Falling in is a death, not a shortcut. The drill is the only hole that goes anywhere.
- **The pit.** From B2 on, each floor has one big round hole somewhere away from the
  drill. It does not close.
- **A care package.** Batteries and the odd plush appear nearby. The crew's theory is
  that it is bait. They take it anyway.

Furnished rooms have the same temper: walking into one usually pays, and sometimes the
room answers with one of the above.

None of this happens around the running drill either: no holes, no blackout, no moving walls,
no fake doorways. The care package still turns up. A broken drill gets no such courtesy.

None of this happens before the Accident. The building was asleep. The crew woke it.

## Floors

Floors are numbered like a car park, because the crew parked a drill in one.

| Floor | What it is |
|-------|-----------|
| B1 | the garage side: tutorial, the Accident, the first (cheap) drill quota. No stall |
| B2 | plain yellow office. The rivals' stall is here and on every floor below |
| B3 and down | the office starts remembering other buildings: a metro station, a mall, a school, a clinic, a resort, a lab, a children's party venue - then round again |

The wallpaper never changes. Only the furniture does. The loot is the same eight things
on every floor; it is just worth more the further it is from daylight, and the drill is
hungrier, and the things in the halls are in a worse mood.

## Dying On The Clock

Death is a scheduling issue, not an ending.

- Go down and a teammate can pick you up. Everything you carried is on the floor.
- Die and you become a small ghost riding on a teammate's shoulder, with full commentary
  rights. Ghosts can ride on a monster's head, and they can still squeak. The squeak is
  real noise where the ghost sits. This is the most dangerous thing in the game.
- The next lamp on the drill pulls the dead back in.
- If everyone goes down, the floor starts over and half the wallet is gone.
- Solo, dying costs the same: half the wallet, same floor, new layout.

## Show, Don't Write

The story is never typed out on screen. No briefing, no notes, no objective text.

| What the player needs to know | How the game says it |
|-------------------------------|----------------------|
| what the company does | the whiteboard in the garage |
| what to bring, how much is left | boxes and outlines on the pallet; lamps on the drill |
| where to go | one pictogram marker with a distance; a pointer on the screen edge |
| what a key does here | key cap + pictograms: `[E] hand`, `[E] box -> drill`, `[E] pickaxe crack` |
| that an action takes time | a ring filling around the key cap |
| what just changed | toasts: coin `+108`, drill `+60`, a check mark |
| what the rivals offer | four key caps, four pictures, four prices |
| how a monster works | one pictogram per studied monster |
| that home is gone | a burnt arch where the door was |
| that it is safe here | a house pictogram that lights up; the lamp and the beacon over the rig's door |
| that the drill broke down | a wrench, a bar running out (the spare battery), a striped cover on the machine |
| how it ended | an arrow up, a coin, a depth, a clock - or a skull and a halved wallet |

Text that remains on purpose: menus and settings, player names, numbers, key names,
squad callouts, the ghost's read-out of what a monster is thinking, the debug overlay -
and the work chat.

## The Work Chat

The company has exactly one piece of working software: the team chat. It survived the
Accident. It is the one or two lines of small text at the top of the screen, and it is
the only voice in the game.

It never tells the story and never gives an objective. It reacts, late and unhelpfully,
in the voice of an office manager who is not down here with you:

| It happens | The chat says |
|------------|---------------|
| phones start ringing | `EVERY PHONE ON THE FLOOR IS RINGING. NOBODY PICK UP` |
| blackout from noise | `TOO LOUD. SOMEBODY FLIPPED THE BREAKER` |
| walls moved | `THE WALLS MOVED. THE WAY BACK IS NOW A RUMOUR` |
| floor holes | `THE FLOOR HAS HOLES NOW. WATCH YOUR STEP` |
| fell into the pit | `THAT PIT WAS NOT ON THE FLOOR PLAN` |
| morale collapse | `DOWNED: MORALE HIT ZERO. SOMEBODY HUG THIS PERSON` |
| knocked down | `DOWNED: WORKPLACE INJURY. A COWORKER CAN PICK YOU UP` |
| died in co-op | `YOU ARE DEAD. BACK ON THE CLOCK AT THE NEXT DRILL LAMP` |
| crawler hit a wall | `BONK. THE CRAWLER KNOCKED ITSELF OUT` |
| scanner cooked | `SCANNER OVERHEATED. THE WARRANTY DID NOT COVER THIS` |
| the drill broke down | `THE DRILL STOPPED AGAIN. THE LAMP IS ON THE SPARE BATTERY` |
| the spare ran out | `THE SPARE BATTERY IS DEAD. NOTHING ABOUT THE DRILL IS SAFE NOW` |
| walked into the auger | `DOWNED: THE POINTY END IS THE FRONT` |
| who shoved whom | `<NAME> SHOVED YOU` - names are always named |

It also posts each monster's rule once, the first time the crew meets one, and the squad
keys once per shift. That is onboarding.

Writing rules for chat lines: capitals, one breath, HR-flat. Never "anomaly", "entity",
"void", "signal", "sector". It is a floor, a pit, a thing, a room, a coworker.

## Tone Rules

1. **The place is scary, the crew is not scared enough.** Play the horror straight and let
   the players be the comedy.
2. **It is a business.** Everything has a price, including being rescued.
3. **One rule per monster.** If it cannot be shouted to a friend in five words, it is too complex.
4. **Death is a punchline, then a comeback.** Nobody sits out for long.
5. **If it needs a sentence, build a prop.** State lives on objects: lamps, boxes, burn marks.
6. **Every tool can be misused on a friend.** If it cannot, add a way.
7. **Somebody is always to blame.** Shared wallet, shared drill, one person who dropped it.
